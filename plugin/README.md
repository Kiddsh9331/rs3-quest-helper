# RS3 Quest Buddy: the in-game plugin (all quests)

The RS3 Quest Helper Alt1 app, ported to Jagex's official plugin API (Lua,
runs inside the game client), with every quick guide on the wiki: about 370 quests, miniquests
and sagas. It started as a 5-quest test build (those five are still what the offline
tests play, from `tests/quests_test.lua`) and tests the three things that
decide whether a full port is worth doing.

The steps in `rs3-quest-buddy/quests.lua` were generated by the Alt1 app's own
parser, so they match what the app shows. Only five have been played in the
beta (The Knight's Sword, Goblin Diplomacy, Vampyre Slayer, Priest in Peril,
Witch's House); the rest have been checked on paper (see "Checking every
quest" below).

## What it does

- **Panel** (Plugins ribbon icon): every quest with live status read from the
  game, no RuneMetrics needed, with All / Started / Not started / Done
  filters and A-D ... U-Z jumps. Pick one and the guide opens at the top:
  status, the game's own **Requirements** (skills with your level, quests,
  quest points, green or red), the quest's items (green when in your
  backpack, red when missing; kit advice like "combat gear and food" in
  grey), then every step with Back / Done. The panel scrolls to a step
  when it changes; **Current step** jumps there; the list comes back where
  you left it; a thin bar on the right shows how far down you are. (Test
  build only) a Debug section with the test tools, including **Probe API**.
  **Settings** switches the card, auto-advance, the path, tile marker,
  teleport hints and recording on or off, and sets the card's detail
  (full / compact) and size (small / medium / large, a share of the screen
  width). Under a missing item: the guide's own note when it has one
  ("Bars can be withdrawn at the anvil"), otherwise "(Grand Exchange)" when
  it's tradeable, or with **Ironman: on** how to make it, followed down
  through its ingredients.
- **Step card** on the game screen: the step, its chat options ("1 / 1"
  chains read as "option 1, then option 1"), its notes indented underneath,
  then what to **Bring** and what to **Get** for this step, each checked
  against your backpack. Very long steps are cut with "(more in the
  panel)"; steps that lean on a wiki picture say so; a list step
  ("Locations:") ticks one sub-bullet per Alt+D. The card sizes itself from
  the screen and stays on it when the window changes; compact mode keeps
  the instruction, chat and missing items. A strip under the title fills
  as you go. Click the title bar to pick it up, click again to drop it.
- **Auto-advance** (Auto: on): interacting with something the current or a
  near-upcoming step names jumps to that step; clicking the step's own
  object ("Search the crate", "Pull the lever") finishes it a few ticks
  later unless the game says nothing happened ("You find nothing", "The
  door is locked"); this needs chat access, and a step that names items
  ("With thread and leather, fix the bellows") waits for the game instead;
  the quest's progress var
  moving finishes the step you were on; picking up an "Obtain/Mine..." item
  ticks that step (also a step or two early); handing a step's items over
  ticks it; getting an item the next step uses ticks the current one;
  reaching what a "follow ... until X" step looks for ticks it; finishing the
  quest ends the guide. Arriving or climbing only ticks a step when that's
  all it asks ("Go upstairs and talk to Doris" waits for the talk), and
  clicking someone you just spoke to doesn't skip ahead to the next time the
  guide names them.
- **World highlights**: NPCs and scenery the step names (trapdoor, blurite
  rocks) get a cyan tile outline and a bobbing arrow above them, the
  nearest three of each name (stairs and ladders: just the nearest one,
  where the dots lead). When the step says which one, only that one is
  outlined: "the lever by the water wheel", "the water controls east of the
  water wheel", "the bookcase on the eastern wall", "the trough to the
  south". "Climb the ladder to the east, then the staircase to the west"
  takes both climbs, each highlighted on its side; "Go upstairs and search
  the drawers" outlines the drawers once you're up, not the stairs back
  down. "Enter the cave" outlines the cave entrance, and the doors and gates
  there only when nothing it names is in sight. On the wrong floor for
  the target (Sir Vyvin is two up), the stairs that way are outlined
  instead; in a dungeon with the target up top, the dots lead back to where
  you came down. Ladders, doors and the like only count for the step you're
  on, so the Lumbridge mill's ladder doesn't skip ahead. Things a step only
  uses as a landmark ("Talk to Adam, north of the well", "the building
  with the anvil", "the throne room", "the ladder behind the altar", "(not
  the portal)") aren't outlined, and clicking one doesn't skip ahead to a
  later step that mentions it that way.
  The step's `{{Map}}` tile gets a beacon until you're within 6 tiles.
- **Dotted path** towards the next place, one dot per tile, walking around
  walls and solid objects (doors count as open). Water and cliffs that are
  part of the ground aren't objects, so the path can't see them.
- **Rough spots**: "Head inside the room to the north" with no map spot
  gets dots to a spot that way from the last place (no marker, a wider
  "arrived"); "Go to the cave" borrows the next step's spot.
- **Teleport hint**: when the next place is far away and a lodestone lands
  much closer, the card and a line under your character say which one, and
  the dots wait until you're there.
- **Dialogue**: after "Talk-to", the conversation's interface is detected and
  remembered, and the step's options turn blue from then on.
- **Recorder**: every session is recorded for offline replay (below).

Not possible with the current API: drawing on the minimap, and true model
outlines (the box is the bounding box the API exposes).

## The three tests

1. **Dialogue reading.** Talk to the step's NPC (for example Squire Asrol on
   The Knight's Sword step 1), and with the options showing press
   **Scan dialogue**. The panel says whether the step's option text was found
   and in which interface, and boxes it in green. The console gets a dump of
   every text the plugin could see. Once found, that interface is remembered
   and options are boxed automatically on later steps.
2. **Wiki fetch.** Press **Test wiki fetch**. It downloads the quest's quick
   guide as raw wikitext. Needs the httpAccess permission (below).
3. **Progress variable.** Nothing to press: play the quest and keep the guide
   on the right step. Every change of the quest's progress variable is logged
   against the step you were on ("Var changes" in the panel, and the console).
   If it moves on most steps, steps can tick themselves in a full version.

## Offline tests (no game needed)

```
pip install lupa
python plugin/tests/run.py
```

Loads the plugin in real Lua 5.4 against a stand-in API (`tests/mock.lua`),
clicks through every feature, replays its own recording (see below) and
prints `ALL PLUGIN TESTS PASSED`. It catches typos and logic bugs, not wrong
guesses about the real API.

### Checking every quest

The plugin's step logic can be run over all ~370 quick guides on paper, to
find what would go wrong in quests nobody has played with it yet (steps that tick too early or never, clicks that skip
ahead, "Get:" lines that aren't items, picture references, NPCs with no
location):

```
python plugin/tools/fetch_wikitext.py wikitext_all.json --cache wt_cache.json
python -m http.server 8090      (from the repo root, then:)
msedge --headless --disable-gpu --dump-dom --virtual-time-budget=60000 ^
  "http://localhost:8090/plugin/tools/dump_guides.html?src=/wikitext_all.json" > dump_all.html
  (copy the JSON out of <pre id="out"> into guides_all.json)
python plugin/tools/build_quests.py guides_all.json --all --cache wiki.json
python plugin/tests/check_quests.py plugin/rs3-quest-buddy/quests.lua --out findings.json
python plugin/tests/run.py --full wiki.json        (the tests, then the checker against tests/check_baseline.json)
```

`check_quests.py --baseline tests/check_baseline.json` compares this run with
the committed counts and lists new bug-class findings (clicks that jump
ahead, linked objects not outlined, travel steps finishing early); after
reviewing a change that moves them, rewrite the file with `--write-baseline`.
Hand-written notes for steps whose solution the wiki only shows as a
picture go in `tools/step_overrides.json` and are merged as sub-bullets.

(`dump_guides.html?src=` needs the JSON served from the same origin, so put
`wikitext_all.json` in the repo root while dumping, and keep it out of git.)
It prints a count per kind of problem with examples; `findings.json` has
every one. `build_quests.py` itself also needs lupa now: it checks what
steps say to get against the wiki.

## Running it in the game

1. Join the plugin beta world once. With the Jagex Launcher, open
   `jagex:launch=runescape,args=[configURI=rs-launch://www.runescape.com/jav_config_beta.ws]`
   (Win+R works); with the standalone client, open
   `rs-launch://www.runescape.com/jav_config_beta.ws`. That creates
   `C:\ProgramData\Jagex\RuneScape-BETA`. In the lobby, click **import save**
   (once per hour). Creating a new character on the beta crashed for us, and
   the five test quests are ones the imported account hasn't started.
   Progress there never touches your live account. To go back to live, fully
   close the game and launcher.
2. Copy the plugin over: `powershell -File plugin\install.ps1`
3. In game, open the console with **Alt + `** and run:
   ```
   prf
   pon rs3-quest-buddy
   ppe rs3-quest-buddy httpAccess
   ppe rs3-quest-buddy chatAccess
   ```
4. Open it from the Plugins ribbon icon. After editing files, re-run the
   install script and `prl rs3-quest-buddy`.

### Shortcuts on the beta world

Typed in the console (Alt + `), with the `!`. They only work on the beta world:

- **Get to an NPC fast:** Ctrl+Shift+click anywhere on the world map,
  minimap or ground to teleport there. `!getcoord` prints where you are.
- **Items for a step:** `!give REDBERRY_PIE` (names from `pan Obj` in the
  console, which copies the full list to your clipboard).
- **Jump a quest's progress:** the panel shows the progress var's name
  (for example `Progress var QUEST_X = 2`). `!getvar <name>` and
  `!setvar <name> <value>` read and set it, which also exercises the var log
  without playing the whole quest.
- **Start over:** re-import your live save (once per hour). Avoid
  `!resetsave`: it sends you back through character creation, which crashed.
- **Avoid `!allinone`:** it completes every quest, which defeats the test.

## Recording a test session

The plugin records what it sees while you play (**Recording: on** in the
panel, on by default). Just play a quest. When something goes wrong, press
**Alt+F** (or **Flag a problem**) right away. That marks the moment in the
recording, so you only have to say what was wrong, not when or where.

Each session is saved in the beta client's `PluginData.db` (the newest 5
are kept, saved every ~6 seconds and on exit). On the developer side:

```
python plugin/tests/read_rec.py          # timeline of the newest session
python plugin/tests/read_rec.py --list   # sessions kept
python plugin/tests/replay.py            # replay it through the current code
```

`read_rec.py` lists the flagged moments and every step you moved by hand
(each one is a step the plugin may have missed), then the full timeline.
`replay.py` feeds the session back through the current plugin code and
reports steps that now complete by themselves, or no longer do. Run it
before and after a fix. `read_rec.py --save file.txt` keeps a copy, and
`replay.py --file file.txt` replays it.

What's recorded: your position, backpack contents, clicks (verb and target),
the quest progress values and statuses, dialogue text on screen, game and NPC
chat lines, and what the plugin decided (steps, highlights, notes). Never
recorded: players' chat (public, private, clan, friends) or the names of
players you click on. The text of an open conversation can include your
character's name, so keep saved copies out of public places.

Send back: what went wrong at each flag, and screenshots for anything visual
(highlights, layout), since the recording can't show what things looked like.

## Release build and mod.io submission

The test build (this folder as checked in) has debug tools and records by
default. The release build is the same code with `edition.lua` switched:
no `devtools.lua`, recording off until a player turns it on, only the
chatAccess permission (quest data is built in, so no httpAccess), and the
wiki credited in the panel. To make the zip:

```
python plugin/tools/package.py --email contact@example.org --version 1.0.0
python plugin/tools/package.py --email ... --no-guidance   (without path, marker and teleport hints)
```

It checks the release copy offline first (`tests/release.lua`; `run.py`
runs the same checks on every test run) and writes
`plugin/dist/<name>-<version>.zip (rs3-quest-buddy-1.0.0.zip)` with `main.lua` and
`plugin.json` at the top level. `LISTING.md` is the draft for the mod.io
page (summary, description, permissions, data protection, EULA statement),
and `CODE-TOUR.md` is a map of the code for reviewing it before submitting.

Still to settle before submitting (from developer.runescape.com/rs/submission/):

- **Walking guidance.** "Automatically indicating where players should
  stand or move" is prohibited. That's aimed at combat, but the dotted path,
  tile marker and teleport hints are close enough to ask about in the
  Plugin Portal Discord (dsc.gg/rspp) first. Whatever the answer, each is a
  setting, and `--no-guidance` builds without them. NPC and object
  highlights are what the hub's Quest Helper already does.
- **Code must be readable and personally reviewed.** AI-assisted code is
  allowed only if you understand it; see `CODE-TOUR.md`.
- **No reading other developers' plugins with AI tools.** Their code and docs
  can't be uploaded to an AI model without the author's permission, so the
  hub's Quest Helper is off limits for this project.
- **Only visible information.** The probe only reads interface components
  the game reports as visible.
- **All platforms.** Uploads must be marked for Windows, Linux, Android and
  iOS. Everything has a button (Alt+D is "Done, next", Alt+F is "Flag a
  problem"), but the panel hasn't been seen on a phone-sized screen yet.
- **Listing:** a contact email (mod.io's form and plugin.json need one; for
  data protection case 1 it needn't be public, but use a separate address
  from your logins anyway), images, and the choices marked in `LISTING.md`.
- **Name:** "RS3 Quest Buddy" (1 to 50 ASCII characters), distinct from the
  hub's "Quest Helper". It's `NAME` in tools/package.py and `name` in
  edition.lua; the Alt1 app keeps its own name, RS3 Quest Helper.
- **Review:** manual; mark the listing hidden until ready and don't change
  the mod.io page during the review's "submission freeze".
